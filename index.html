Before you drive

You're finishing on nights.

Seventeen hours awake slows your reactions about as much as a 0.05 blood alcohol reading. Twenty-four hours is closer to 0.10. After a night shift you're at the bottom of your body clock, on a quiet road, in the worst part of the day for it — and the first sign of a microsleep is usually waking up.

  • Sleep before you drive, not after. Even a few hours at camp beats pushing through.
  • If you must go, a 20 minute nap and a coffee first buys you about an hour — no more.
  • Share the driving, or wear the extra night and leave in daylight.
  • Pulling over for a sleep is never the wrong call.
